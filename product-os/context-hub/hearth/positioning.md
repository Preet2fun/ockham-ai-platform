# Hearth — Positioning

## Draft positioning statement

> For US restaurant groups with 2–20 delivery-heavy locations and no analyst
> on staff, **Hearth** is the operations console that explains itself. Its AI
> analyst reads your own orders, menu, deliveries and payments, then tells you
> what is happening, why, and what to do next, with the evidence to show for
> it. POS and back-office suites hand you reports to interpret. Hearth hands
> you the interpretation.

## The wedge

The wedge is **the analyst the group can't afford to hire**, built into the
console the operators already work in. It is not a POS replacement and not a
BI tool. A group at this size will not buy a warehouse, a dashboard tool and
an analyst. Hearth closes that gap for the price of software.

## Core pitches

### Owner (economic buyer)

> Every week you find out too late which location, item or delivery window
> cost you money. Hearth's analyst reads all of your locations' data every day
> and tells you in plain English what moved, why, and the one thing to fix
> first.

### Operations director / GM (daily user)

> Instead of exporting reports and building spreadsheets, ask: "Why were
> deliveries late at lunch?" You get the answer, the numbers behind it, and a
> next step, scoped to the location you're looking at.

## Trust line: grounded, never guessed

> Every answer shows the numbers it came from. If Hearth doesn't have the data
> to answer, it says so. It never makes a change on its own.

"Shows the numbers it came from" is the phrase. Grounding and refusing to
guess are the product, not a disclaimer.

## Metric rules

- Talk about **decisions made sooner**, for example "spot the lunch delivery
  bottleneck the same day, not at month-end." Avoid vague "AI insights."
- **Never claim forecast accuracy** before it is measured. Forecasting is H4.
- **Never say "in seconds."** Describe what the answer contains, not how fast
  it arrives.
- **Never imply cross-tenant benchmarking** ("restaurants like yours…") until
  it exists. Every answer is about the tenant's own data only.
